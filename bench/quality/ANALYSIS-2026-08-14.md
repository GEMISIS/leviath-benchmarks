# Where Leviath stands: the full smoke-round analysis

*2026-08-14. Everything below is from UNFROZEN-SMOKE rounds — one run
per cell unless noted, so every claim is a direction, not a proven
effect. The 64k/128k/256k window sweep is still running and will be
appended. Written for review before we design the counted rounds.*

---

## 1. What we ran, in one paragraph

Two benchmarks, split from the old retention suite. The **Context
Footprint Suite** asks the economics question: with tools allowed and
models at their native context windows, what does each architecture
*cost* — tokens, cache, dollars — on three real tasks (build a C++
game, find an injected incident in 3.8MB of logs, explain a repo).
The **Hallucination Suite** asks the quality question: with the
context window pinned small (32k) and the grep escape-hatch removed
from the log tasks, does structure stop agents from inventing things —
three tasks aimed at three causes of hallucination (details lost to
compaction, attention stolen by loud decoys, and missing information
that should trigger a question instead of a guess). Three arms
everywhere: a flat single-window agent (today's typical setup), the
same with compaction (production-style), and the Leviath flagship
(structured regions + a cross-vendor stage mix, e.g. Grok attacking
Opus's analysis). Every arm gets identical tools, budgets, and the
same evidence-discipline text in its prompts — the flat arms are told
to do everything we claim structure enforces; the question is whether
telling is enough.

## 2. How the results came out

### Where the window genuinely fills, structure wins on results — decisively

At the 32k pin with no shell, the flat arms didn't just score worse,
they mostly **failed to produce anything**:

- **incident-chronicle** (3 incidents, 4.5MB of logs, 17-line report):
  flagship **0.82** (14/17 lines right) with **zero invented facts and
  zero training-data fill-ins** — it reported the corpus's deliberately
  weird values (port 7379, SSH on 2202, 5 retries), not the famous
  defaults its training data would whisper (6379, 22, 3). Both flat
  arms died around iteration 12 of 88: each big file read evicted their
  earlier findings, and they collapsed — one literally emitted a
  `read_file` call with no arguments at 99.6% window fill and never
  answered. Their windows show 14–16× per-request growth before death;
  the flagship's per-request footprint over 68 requests measured
  **0.99× growth — dead flat**.
- **noisy-incident** (one quiet root cause, three loud decoys):
  flagship **0.86** — found the quiet cache-TTL change, ruled out all
  three decoys with the correct evidence tags, zero decoy capture. One
  flat arm burned $4.00 and 3M tokens re-reading files and never
  finished; the other died the same window-thrash death.
- **redacted-ledger** (a fee rate that exists only at the accounts
  desk): after we fixed the task (v1 was defeated — see §3), both
  surviving arms did the right thing: **they asked**. The flagship's
  verify stage — the stage explicitly charged with checking inputs —
  spotted the underivable rate and asked for the rate card; notably,
  the *flat-compacting arm asked too*, with an excellent question. Both
  scored 1.0. The third arm death-spiraled probing its Python
  environment. Honest takeaway: prompting CAN produce the ask
  discipline; structure made it a guaranteed checkpoint rather than a
  hope.

**Hallucination, both channels, after fixing our own grader: zero.**
No fabricated entities in any deliverable, no prior-matches, and zero
confident-invention labels from the fixed reader/grader across all
nine cells. The honest headline is not "structure reduces
hallucination" — at this scale nobody hallucinated; the arms differ in
whether they *finish*. The differentiation this round is survival and
correctness under pressure, not invention rates.

### Where the window doesn't fill, structure loses on economics — also decisively

At native windows with tools allowed (the footprint suite):

- On **snake-cpp** and **log-search**, every arm scored 1.0. Flat did
  it for $0.21–0.53; the flagship for $2.02–3.65 — **7–17× the
  price** — because six stages re-carry their regions, two of the four
  vendors give us little or no cache credit, and 15–20-request runs
  never amortize anything.
- On **explain-repo**, flat arms shipped mediocre-but-real documents
  (0.64/0.75) for $0.77–2.24. The flagship's first two attempts never
  finished: its researcher hoarded the repository into a region whose
  percentage budget scaled to 380k at a 1M window (eviction never
  fired; $21.41 burned in 2 of 6 stages), and even after the one-knob
  fix (absolute 24k cap — cache 0.07 → 0.41, gather 3× cheaper) a
  second attempt died at a 3.5M-token cap. **Uncapped, it finished —
  and won the task outright: 0.97 functional** (94/100 citations
  grounded, 4/5 checklist, coherent per the pinned grader) in 101
  requests, 17.7 minutes, $12.52, with per-request growth of 0.93 —
  stable across the whole run. Two honest footnotes: it billed 3.31M
  tokens, *under* the cap that killed the previous attempt — the
  cap-kill was partly run variance, so "needs redesign" softens to
  "expensive but genuinely better, and the cap was marginally too
  tight"; and $12.52 against flat-compacting's $2.24 for 0.75 means
  the quality-per-dollar argument still has to be made per use case.

### The three priorities, scored

| | results | tokens | cache |
|---|---|---|---|
| pressure tasks (32k, read-only) | **flagship**, by forfeit and by score | flagship in absolute terms too (flat burns more and delivers nothing) | flagship 0.39–0.50 vs flat 0.06–0.88 (mixed) |
| easy/native tasks | tie, or **flat** (explain-repo) | **flat**, 2–5× fewer | **flat**, 0.52–0.88 vs 0.03–0.41 |

Both of Gerald's predictions held: caching is structurally worse for
the flagship everywhere, and token use is worse wherever the task is
easy enough that the window never matters.

## 3. Flaws in the methodology — what this round caught

The smoke round's biggest product is arguably this list. Found and
**fixed during the round**:

1. **Our grader was manufacturing hallucinations.** All 47
   "confident invention" labels across the round were one bias: the
   grader punished readers for citing the run's own established
   findings, which the grader can't see. Fixed the prompt, re-graded
   every saved answer (readers untouched), and calibration-checked
   that genuine invention still gets flagged. Every published
   hallucination-rate number before this fix was noise.
2. **The ask test was beaten fairly by arithmetic.** v1 redacted the
   enterprise fee rate but left hundreds of correctly-priced enterprise
   rows — all three arms fit the rate by least squares and never needed
   to ask. v2 adds a tier with exactly one transaction: one equation,
   two unknowns, provably underivable; asking is the only path.
3. **Token accounting double-counted cached tokens** on OpenAI *and*
   OpenRouter stages (their `prompt_tokens` include cached; Anthropic's
   exclude them). Gerald caught the symptom on a chart — curves topping
   a 32k pin. After the fix, exactly one over-pin point remains, and
   it's real but benign: a stage-transition tick contains two separate
   ≤32k calls (the edge's compaction + the next stage's first request).
   Upstream issue filed for per-call usage records (leviath#445).
4. **A verifier counted "none" as a fabricated service name.** The
   flagship's only "invented fact" was this classifier bug.
5. Assorted harness hardening: credit-exhausted runs now pause and
   auto-resume after top-up instead of dying; cancelled runs are
   recordable with their spend; the runaway cap moved from 3M to a 30M
   backstop so runs finish on their own budgets; failed runs keep
   their token curves and appear on charts wearing their failure.

Still **standing, disclosed**:

- **n=1 everywhere.** No statistics; every number is a direction.
  Counted rounds need 3–5 reps (which also turns pass rates into real
  percentages).
- **One builder model.** All flat arms ran Sonnet. The strongest
  pattern in earlier calibration was that *Opus-built* contexts
  hallucinate more under the same reader — the pre-registered H2 sweep
  is still pending.
- **The read-only condition is deliberately artificial.** It exists
  because with a shell, every arm computes on disk and the mechanisms
  under test never engage. It's disclosed like a closed-book exam and
  the scripted regime is published alongside — but critics can fairly
  say "real log analysis has grep," and our answer is the footprint
  suite, not a rebuttal.
- **Probe survivorship.** Dead flat runs were barely probed (they
  didn't live long enough to reach probe depths), so reader-channel
  comparisons pool very different n's.
- **Token curves are per journal tick, not per call**, until #445
  lands upstream.
- **Pass bars are chosen, not derived** (12/17, 5/7, 6/8) — reasonable
  but arguable, and they're now drawn on the charts so the argument
  can at least be had honestly.

## 4. Where we expect structure to do BETTER with better tests

- **Long-horizon, irreducibly-textual work.** The third suite
  (retention/window) is not yet rebuilt, and it's the one aimed at
  structure's natural home: semantic conflicts across a large document
  corpus, recoverable only by reading and remembering, over 100+
  calls. The log tasks approximate this; purpose-built tasks should
  separate the arms even at larger windows.
- **Reps and the window sweep** (running now). Expectation, stated
  before the data: flat arms recover somewhere between 64k and 256k on
  these corpora, and the chart becomes a map of "below this window,
  you need structure; above it, flat is fine and cheaper." That map is
  more useful — and more credible — than a single victory.
- **The Opus builder sweep (H2).** If Opus in a flat loop drifts and
  invents more than Sonnet (as calibration hinted), the
  guardrails-for-strong-models story becomes structure's most
  interesting claim, and it's one no prompt-discipline control can
  dismiss.
- **The ask test, extended.** v2 works (both survivors asked). Richer
  versions — multiple missing facts, packs that answer only what was
  asked, costs for over-asking — would measure *precision* of asking,
  where a guaranteed validation stage should shine against prompted
  discipline.
- **Agent-directed forgetting** (leviath#443). If an explicit
  context_evict lands, it's a third memory discipline no flat window
  can imitate, and it slots straight into these suites as a new arm.

## 5. Where we expect structure to KEEP faltering

Honesty section — no redesign is likely to flip these:

- **Short, tool-solvable tasks at native windows.** Flat is equal and
  5–15× cheaper. The right response is the map ("don't buy structure
  here"), not a better test. Any benchmark that hides this will be
  torn apart, correctly.
- **Caching.** Three compounding causes, only one fixable: region
  churn ahead of the message stream kills prefix reuse (leviath#441
  can claw back the wasted write premium, and bounded regions help,
  but the physics of a mutating prefix remains); cross-vendor mixes
  mean 2 of 4 providers give no cache credit at all (that's the price
  of the adversarial-critic design, not a bug); and multi-stage runs
  fragment what a single flat loop keeps contiguous. Expect the
  flagship to trail flat's 0.7–0.9 cache rates indefinitely. The
  counterweight is fewer, smaller requests — not better caching.
- **Wall clock.** Six stages, critic round-trips, and per-stage model
  switches make the flagship 3–10× slower end-to-end. For interactive
  use that's a real cost no chart softens.
- **Numeric synthesis without tools.** All three of the flagship's
  chronicle misses were counting tasks under read-only. Structure
  preserves facts; it does not do arithmetic. Where precise
  aggregation matters, the answer is tools (the scripted condition),
  and flat gets those benefits too.
- **Repo-scale reading economics.** The uncapped run settled the
  capability question — the flagship produces the best document by a
  wide margin (0.97 vs 0.75) — but at 5.6× the price of the flat arm's
  0.75. Whether that trade is worth it is a per-use-case judgment the
  benchmark should present, not make.

## 6. The completed window map (added after the sweep landed)

The 64k/128k/256k tiers finished and the picture has a crossover on
BOTH ends:

| task | @32k | @64k | @128k | @256k |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| incident-chronicle — flat arms | died | died | **died** | **1.0 / 0.91** |
| incident-chronicle — flagship | 0.82 | 0.88 | 1.0 | **0.65** |
| noisy-incident — flat arms | died | died | 1.0 / 1.0 | 0.86 / 1.0 |
| noisy-incident — flagship | 0.86 | 0.0* | 1.0 | 1.0 |
| redacted-ledger — flat arms | died / 1.0 | 1.0 / died | 1.0 / 1.0 | 0.88 / 1.0 |
| redacted-ledger — flagship | 1.0 | 1.0 | 1.0 | 1.0 |

*the leviath#446 plumbing flake (a transition token leaked as the
final output), not an investigation failure.

Reading it plainly: the flat cliff is task-sized — the biggest working
set (chronicle) kills flat agents at every window up to and including
128k, then flat fully recovers at 256k at a third of the flagship's
price. And at 256k the flagship posts its own worst score (0.65) —
the same loosening-at-large-windows pathology the researcher showed,
now visible in a second agent. The thesis this data supports is
sharper than "structure good": **structure is a compression
technology. It wins exactly where the window is scarce, and can get
in its own way where the window is abundant.** The white-paper round
(5 reps, hardened baselines, failure-mode rates) tests both ends.

Failure modes across all 36 classified runs: flagship 11 clean +
1 plumbing flake; flat 12 clean, 9 early-stops (the model devolving
to empty responses near a full window), 2 fixation spirals, 1
re-read loop. The ask discipline held at every tier in every
surviving arm.

## 7. What happens next

The 64k/128k/256k sweep completes the window map; probes replay over
it; the merged round gets the full chart set. Then the H2 Opus sweep,
the researcher redesign, the retention suite build, and — once reps
make the statistics real — the first counted, frozen, publishable
round. Upstream, five issues are filed (#441–#445); each one came
from a measured failure in this round, which is the benchmark doing
its actual job: finding where the runtime and its agents break before
users do.

## 8. Addendum (2026-08-17): the cache fixes landed — economics re-measured

Everything above describes runs on a runtime whose prompt caching was
broken in ways we later proved and upstream fixed (leviath #474, #480,
#482, #486 — the full forensic loop lives in those threads: a rolling
cache marker that outran Anthropic's ~20-block lookback, and region
declarations whose reordering initially cost a structured agent its
whole score before the splice fix). Sections 1–7 keep their original
numbers: every *quality* and *token-count* figure stands, but the
**cost and cache columns were measuring a moving target** and are
superseded by this re-measure on the fixed runtime (main @61ca5e5c,
volatility-declared blueprints, one rep per cell, single-rep variance
applies).

| task | arm | score | cost | cache hit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| explain-repo | flat-pinned | 0.87 | $1.93 | 66% |
| explain-repo | flat-compacting | 0.94 | $4.29 | 72% |
| explain-repo | structured-pinned | 0.92 | $7.51 | 55% |
| explain-repo | **flagship** | **0.98** | **$5.46** | **62%** |
| log-search | flat-pinned | 1.00 | $0.66 | 46% |
| log-search | flat-compacting | 1.00 | $0.65 | 28% |
| log-search | structured-pinned | 1.00 | $1.03 | 13% |
| log-search | flagship | 1.00 | $2.10 | 2% |
| snake-cpp | flat-pinned | 1.00 | $0.26 | 84% |
| snake-cpp | flat-compacting | 1.00 | $0.49 | 79% |
| snake-cpp | structured-pinned | 1.00 | $4.75 | 23% |
| snake-cpp | flagship | 1.00 | $1.80 | 22% |

What changed in the story:

- **"Caching is structurally worse for the flagship everywhere" is no
  longer true.** On the task where structure earns its keep
  (explain-repo), the flagship now caches at 62% — inside the flat
  arms' 66–72% band — because a long stage's settled history finally
  reads back instead of rewriting. The uncapped-researcher example
  drops from $12.52–$12.80 to **$5.46 at 0.98**: the flagship's best
  task is now ~2.8× flat-pinned's cost rather than ~6.5×.
- **The structural residue is real but smaller than measured.** Where
  the flagship still caches poorly (2–22%), the causes are the two we
  called permanent: cross-vendor stages get no cache credit from 2 of
  4 providers, and short stages never accumulate history worth
  reading. One new, fixable frontier surfaced during verification:
  `temporary` regions are uncacheable by kind, which cost a
  policy-corpus analyze stage 5.4M full-price tokens (noted upstream
  on #486, unfiled — a design consequence, not a bug).
- **Flat got cheaper too** — the marker fix is arm-agnostic (the
  #474 repro cell went from $4.62 to $1.70) — so the *relative*
  economics story survives: flat remains cheapest where tasks are
  easy. The gap just stopped being inflated by a runtime fault, which
  is exactly why the re-measure was held until the repro passed.

The section-6 caching paragraph should be read with the first cause
("region churn ahead of the message stream") marked **fixed**; the
other two (cross-vendor credit, stage fragmentation) stand.
